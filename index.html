Race data, burned into your footage.

An iPhone and iPad video editor for sailors: sync GPX or Vakaros tracks to your clips, design the data layer — strip, card, big numbers or instrument gauges — and export a new video.

Your first edit

  1. Open the sample project on the first screen to see it work with two boats and a short drone clip. Or import your own tracks (Files, or Share → Sail Overlay) and add clips from Photos.
  2. Design: pick a layout, a look (Smoke, Broadcast, Bare), an accent colour, the size, units and an optional title.
  3. Sync: clips are placed by their recording time. If a tack on screen and the dip in speed don't match, nudge the clip by seconds.
  4. Cut and frame: keep the full clip or tap a detected tack, jibe or top speed to cut a highlight; go vertical 9:16; speed up if you like.
  5. Export: render, then save to Photos, post to an Instagram Story or share.

Questions

Which files work?

GPX from any app, watch or tracker (every track in a file is a boat), and Vakaros Atlas .vkx logs, which add heel, pitch, heading, depth and wind. Video from the Photos library: phone, action camera or drone, landscape or portrait.

My clip says it is outside the tracks.

The camera's clock and the tracks don't overlap. Check the date and time zone on the camera; use the ±1 h buttons for a time-zone mix-up, or "Move it to the start of the tracks" and nudge from there.

Where does the wind direction come from?

From the boats' tacks and jibes. When the tracks only show the wind's axis, the editor asks which way it blew. You can always set it by hand in the Wind tab, or turn it off.

The Vakaros heel looks wrong.

The app corrects for how the unit is mounted using stretches of steady sailing. A log recorded mostly at the dock may not have enough of that; please send it to us.

Is anything uploaded?

No. Footage and tracks stay on your device. Map tiles for the sailing area are downloaded to draw the map. Sending tracks to wakeroute.com for a results page is optional and only happens if you tap it.
